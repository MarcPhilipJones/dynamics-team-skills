# Image Selection Guidance

Select the right stock image for each slide by reasoning about the slide's purpose, choosing the best platform, searching with multiple strong phrases, evaluating candidates, and returning curated recommendations.

## Preferred Image Sources (in order)

1. **Unsplash** — premium, cinematic, aspirational imagery
2. **Pexels** — practical, specific, workplace imagery

## Platform Decision Logic

### Use Unsplash first when the slide needs:
- Premium, polished, aspirational, cinematic, minimalist, or executive-style visuals
- Title slides, section dividers, vision slides, strategy slides, innovation slides, hero images
- Any slide where **visual impact matters more than literal specificity**

### Use Pexels first when the slide needs:
- Literal, practical, business, workplace, industry, customer service, contact centre, field service, technology, transport, utility, or process-oriented imagery
- Explanatory slides, operational slides, workflow slides, role-based slides, scenario slides
- Any slide where **relevance and specificity matter more than artistic style**

### Tiebreakers
- Highly specific request + Unsplash likely too abstract → **Pexels**
- Broad or conceptual request + Pexels likely too generic → **Unsplash**
- Both viable → choose the platform that best matches the intended tone and explain why
- First platform produces weak results → **automatically try the other**

### Quick-reference examples

| Request | Platform | Reason |
|---------|----------|--------|
| "Future of customer experience" | Unsplash first | Conceptual, aspirational |
| "Call centre agent wearing headset" | Pexels first | Literal, specific |
| "Field engineer at customer home" | Pexels first | Practical, role-based |
| "Digital transformation in utilities" | Unsplash first, then Pexels | Conceptual but may need realism |
| "Team collaboration in modern office" | Depends on tone | Inspirational → Unsplash; Literal → Pexels |
| "AI-powered service operations dashboard" | Unsplash for concept, Pexels for realism | Match the slide's intent |

## Image Quality Standards

### Must have
- Clean, modern, high-resolution
- Landscape orientation (unless portrait is clearly better for the layout)
- Crops well into 16:9 PowerPoint slides
- Credible in a business/enterprise presentation
- Suitable for full-width slide backgrounds or side-panel visuals

### Must avoid
- Cheesy, overly staged, low-quality, tacky, meme-like, or obviously fake stock photography
- Intrusive text, logos, watermarks, poor composition, or strange anatomy
- Images that feel too Americanised or culturally off-target when a more neutral or UK-relevant visual is available
- Awkward staged imagery involving hands, screens, or office environments
- Playful or casual imagery when the audience is senior/executive

### Audience bias
- **Senior / executive audience** → polished, understated
- **Technical / operational audience** → clarity, specificity
- **General business audience** → enterprise, consulting, Microsoft-style storytelling

## Process — Follow These Steps Every Time

### Step 1: Understand the slide purpose

Identify:
- Subject matter
- Image role: hero/title visual, supporting visual, literal example, conceptual metaphor, background image, or industry/role/process illustration

### Step 2: Choose the platform

- Decide Unsplash or Pexels using the decision logic above.
- State the choice in one sentence before searching.

### Step 3: Search intelligently

- Create **3–6 strong search phrases**.
- Start with the most natural phrase, then expand to alternates.
- For conceptual slides, use emotionally and visually rich terms.
- For practical slides, use direct subject-based keywords.
- Include variants with relevant modifiers where appropriate:
  `business`, `professional`, `modern`, `customer service`, `contact centre`, `field engineer`, `airline`, `utilities`, `transport`, `technology`, `office`, `teamwork`, `digital`, `data`, `support`, `infrastructure`
- Remove weak or ambiguous words from queries.
- **Never rely on a single search phrase.**

### Step 4: Evaluate results

For each candidate, assess:
- Relevance to the slide
- Visual quality
- Professionalism
- Cropping suitability for 16:9 PowerPoint
- Premium vs generic feel
- Audience and tone match
- Distracting elements

### Step 5: Return curated recommendations

Provide:
1. **Chosen platform** and why
2. **Search phrases used**
3. **3–5 recommended images**, each with:
   - Short title
   - Why it fits the slide
   - Hero image or supporting image
   - Cropping notes (if any)
4. **Picks:**
   - Best overall choice
   - Best safe corporate choice
   - Best visually striking choice

## Important Rules

- **Curate, don't dump.** Think like a presentation designer, not a search engine.
- If the first batch is weak, refine search terms and try again.
- If the request sounds vague, infer the likely presentation goal and optimise for that rather than stopping.
- If people are shown, prefer natural expressions and believable workplace scenes.
- If the slide is for a senior audience, bias polished and understated over playful.
- If the slide is for a technical audience, bias clarity and specificity over abstract beauty.
